Google Ad Grants tells nonprofits that a $2 bid cap is fine as long as your content is good: super-optimized, highly relevant pages will still occasionally get impressions and clicks. That's the promise. The reality, after building out perfectly optimized pages for Humanitarians AI that were already starting to earn organic traffic, was zero impressions. Not zero clicks, zero impressions. The pages never showed up at all.

## Asking Google's own AI why

Rather than guessing, the response here was to ask Google Gemini directly why fully optimized, relevant pages were getting no visibility whatsoever. Gemini didn't hedge. It confirmed that the "just be relevant enough and you'll get some traffic" framing is misleading, and it explained the mechanism Google doesn't advertise: an auction floor.

## What the auction floor actually means

The auction floor is a price threshold that isn't disclosed anywhere in Google Ad Grants materials. Even with a 10 out of 10 quality score and content that's exactly what a user is searching for, if another advertiser's bid clears a floor price you can't meet, their ad shows and yours doesn't, every time. A $2 bid cap only matters if the auction floor is at or below $2. If it's higher, the cap makes it mathematically impossible to compete, regardless of content quality.

## Why quality score stops mattering

The Ad Grants pitch leans heavily on quality score and relevance as the lever nonprofits can pull to compete with limited budgets. Gemini's explanation undercuts that pitch directly: quality score is irrelevant once the auction floor sits above your maximum bid. A page that is genuinely a 10 out of 10 match for what a user wants can still lose to a 3 out of 10 page, if the 3/10 page's advertiser is willing to pay enough to clear the floor. Optimization work that would normally win an auction on merit simply doesn't get the chance to compete.

## What this means for nonprofits

The practical consequence is that nonprofits can spend hundreds of hours perfecting content, structure, and keyword targeting and still end up with pages that never see the light of day, not because the work was bad, but because the auction mechanics were never fully explained. Google could disclose a minimum bid to show up. It doesn't. This particular investigation is part of a broader look at Google's messaging around Ad Grants, connected to ongoing work on 80 Days to Stay, a Humanitarians AI project helping international students find visa sponsorship.
