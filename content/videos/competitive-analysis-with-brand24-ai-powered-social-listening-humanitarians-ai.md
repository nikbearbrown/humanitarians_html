Knowing that people are talking about your brand online is easy. Knowing whether that conversation is helping or hurting you, and what your competitor's version of the same conversation looks like, is a different and much harder problem. This tutorial walks through Brand24, a social listening and analytics tool, using a real project built around Coca-Cola and Pepsi to show what that harder problem actually looks like once AI is doing the sorting.

## What Brand24 actually does

Brand24 is a website that performs social listening and monitoring, providing insight into brand mentions and online reputation. Its AI connection isn't incidental: it uses AI-powered sentiment analysis to categorize each mention as positive, neutral, or negative, applies natural language processing to make sense of large volumes of text across social media, blogs, forums, and other platforms, and uses AI algorithms to filter and prioritize which mentions actually matter, so a brand's attention goes to actionable data rather than noise.

## Setting up a project

Getting started follows a familiar pattern: sign up, with a 15-day free trial before any pricing kicks in, then set up a project by entering the keywords and key phrases you want tracked. In the demo, that means entering the target brand's name and common variants, "Coca-Cola" and "Coke," alongside a direct competitor, "Pepsi," plus a related hashtag. You also choose which languages to track mentions in, English in this case, before creating the project and landing on the dashboard.

## Reading the dashboard

Once a project exists, the dashboard organizes everything into a set of distinct sections: a mentions summary, AI insights, deeper analysis, an AI brand assistant, topic analysis, a comparison view, sources, and influencers. The mentions and reach graph is the first thing worth understanding, since it can be filtered by day, week, or month to show how a brand's presence has trended over time, including growth and decline. Below that, individual mentions are broken out by source, Facebook, Instagram, X, TikTok, news outlets, podcasts, forums, and blogs, each mention showing where and when it happened and whether it read as a positive or negative brand moment. One example surfaced in the demo: a mention that Coca-Cola had gifted a commemorative bottle in a political context, illustrating how specific and current these captured mentions can be. The negative sentiment view narrows the same data down to just the mentions read as negative, pulling in examples from platforms like YouTube, Quora, and Reddit, which is exactly where most brands want to focus first when trying to understand where they're losing ground.

## What the AI insights section adds

The summary section gives an overall count of mentions, reach, and interactions, split between positive and negative sentiment and filterable by the same date ranges. The AI insights section goes further, generating a written narrative interpretation of the brand's current standing, its cultural impact, and recent trends, not just raw counts. One concrete figure surfaced in the demo shows the kind of scale this tool is built to track: a substantial increase in overall mentions from 917 in a previous period to 37.9 thousand in the current one. That kind of jump is exactly the sort of signal a SWOT analysis needs, and Brand24 pairs the observation with generated recommendations, in this case suggesting a targeted marketing campaign built around a brand's connection to a seasonal moment like the holidays, based on a spike in mentions during that period.

## Comparison as the actual competitive step

The analysis section goes deeper still, surfacing which public profiles are driving the most mentions and reach for a brand. But the feature that makes this a genuinely competitive tool, rather than just a monitoring one, is the comparison page, where a brand can be set directly against a competitor, Coca-Cola against Pepsi in this case, to see side by side how each is performing and where one is pulling ahead.

## What this adds up to

Across the whole walkthrough, a few consistent lessons emerge about what actually drives brand performance in this kind of analysis: using high-impact hashtags effectively, collaborating with influencers for engagement that reads as authentic rather than paid, staying attached to trending topics, and understanding sentiment in enough depth to know not just how many mentions exist but how people feel about them. The broader case for a tool like this rests on three things: it builds brand awareness and visibility, it surfaces genuine customer insight into how a brand is actually being talked about, and it gives real competitive intelligence, the ability to monitor a rival's campaigns and strategies directly rather than guessing at them.

## Key takeaways

- Brand24 combines AI sentiment analysis and NLP to sort large volumes of social, blog, and forum mentions into positive, neutral, and negative categories.
- A project is set up around specific keywords and competitor names, with a 15-day free trial before paid pricing applies.
- The dashboard's mentions, sources, and sentiment breakdowns let you trace exactly where and how a brand is being discussed across platforms.
