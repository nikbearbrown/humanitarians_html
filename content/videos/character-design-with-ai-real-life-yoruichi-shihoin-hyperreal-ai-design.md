Turning a stylized anime character into something that looks like it was shot on a real camera is one of the harder tests for an AI image generator. It is easy to get a generic fantasy portrait; it is much harder to keep the specific details that make a character recognizable while still convincing the eye that a real photographer was standing there. This piece walks through exactly that attempt, reimagining a fast-moving, golden-eyed anime swordfighter as a hyperreal photographic subject.

The published prompt is worth reading closely because it shows how much of the final image comes from precise, layered description rather than a single vague request. It specifies a tall, athletic build, golden eyes, dark skin, and long, flowing purple hair. It calls for a sleek, sleeveless outfit made of white and black robes. It names a mixed-race Asian identity for the subject. And it anchors the whole thing stylistically with two phrases that do a lot of work: "hyperrealist" and "in the style of National Geographic." That second phrase in particular is a common trick for pushing a generator away from illustration and toward something that reads as documentary photography, with natural light, candid posing, and photojournalistic texture.

## What the oref parameter is doing

Alongside the descriptive prompt, the generation uses Midjourney's omni-reference feature, written as `--oref` followed by a reference image URL, with an omni-weight value set through `--ow`. This is the mechanism that lets a specific reference image influence the final render rather than leaving everything to text description alone. The omni-weight value of 155 sits on the lighter end of the scale, meaning the reference nudges the result without locking it rigidly to the source image. That is a deliberate middle ground: strong enough to guide the pose, coloring, or composition, but loose enough to let the detailed text prompt still shape most of the outcome. The aspect ratio is set to 9:16, a vertical format built for short-form video and mobile viewing rather than a traditional landscape portrait.
