## The shape underneath the silliness

Strip away the bugs and the splashing, and "Five Little Speckled Frogs" is a countdown. Every verse subtracts one frog and restates the total, out loud, in rhythm. That is the same basic move a child makes when counting backward from five, except here it is wrapped in a call-and-response game with a punchline every time: "yum, yum," and then a splash. The repetition is not filler. It is the mechanism. A child does not learn "five minus one is four" by being told; they learn it by hearing the same sentence five times with only one number changing, until the pattern becomes obvious on its own.

That is also why the song resists a rushed retelling. Skip a verse and the countdown breaks. The whole piece depends on going through every step in order, which is a small, almost invisible discipline dressed up as a nursery rhyme.
