Writing a full prompt sheet for an AI-generated music video by hand can run twenty or thirty pages once you account for sequencing, style, and shot-to-shot continuity. This video introduces Muzak, a command-based tool built to compress that work into a single instruction, turning lyrics, images, or both into ready-to-use image and video prompts.

## What Muzak is built to do

Given lyrics, reference images, or both, Muzak generates the initial image prompts for a music video and then the sequenced video prompts that follow from those images. Like every GPT-style tool built this way, it's backed by real research, in this case research into how professionals actually sequence music videos, condensed into five to ten pages of rules behind each command so the user doesn't have to write that detail out manually every time.

## Commands instead of manual prompt-writing

Muzak is entirely command-based. A command like "dance" pulls in the tool's researched rules on scripting, sequencing, and music video structure to produce a full set of prompts. Commands can be combined with style modifiers, dance bluegrass, hip-hop, folk, for example, to shift the tone toward a specific genre feel. Users can start from lyrics alone, or upload reference images of the look they're going for and let the tool build around that starting point.

## Why sequencing matters

The important property of Muzak's output is that the prompts are sequenced, not generated in isolation. When the goal is to tell a story, image one needs to connect to image two, which needs to connect to image three, and so on. Because the output is entirely text, the results are legible even before generating anything: you can read the prompts, get a sense of where the sequence is heading, and decide whether to run with it, regenerate it, or hand it to another tool entirely.

## First drafts, not final drafts

Like every creative AI workflow, Muzak's prompts are explicitly framed as starting points rather than finished work. They get you close, and from there the normal creative loop takes over: refine, refine, and refine again until the result matches what you actually want. The tool is also actively evolving, with new commands and functions added over time based on what does and doesn't work in practice, while existing functionality is generally kept rather than removed.
