Teaching a three or four year old about the five senses usually means naming them once and hoping it sticks. This song takes a different approach: it hands kids a simple, repeatable chorus, then walks through each sense one at a time with examples pulled straight from a child's day, the sun outside, a bell ringing, cookies baking, so the vocabulary attaches to something they already know rather than to an abstract list.

## Building the chorus first

The song opens with the full set in one breath: "I can see, I can hear, I can smell things far and near. I can taste, I can touch, I can do and learn so much." That chorus does double duty. It is catchy enough to sing on its own, and it previews the structure of everything that follows, so kids know from the first line that five things are coming and roughly what order they will arrive in. Repeating that chorus between verses gives the whole song a shape kids can predict, which matters for preschool attention spans.

## Sight and sound, anchored to real things

Sight gets grounded in things a child can actually picture: the sun bright in the sky, stars at night, a teddy bear, the people around them. Hearing follows the same pattern, a bird singing, a bell going "ding," music loud and clear. Neither verse tries to explain how eyes or ears work. Instead, each one gives the child three or four concrete examples and trusts that naming enough instances teaches the category, which is how most preschoolers actually build vocabulary.

## Smell, taste, and touch

The smell verse leans on cookies baking and flowers, then adds a useful nuance for a kids' song: "some smell good and some not," which quietly introduces the idea that a sense can carry information, not just pleasant sensation. Taste follows with pie and French fries, plus the pairing "sweet and sour, hot or cold," giving kids contrasting word pairs rather than a single flat description. Touch closes the set with a teddy bear's softness, snow's chill, and a warm blanket, again leaning on textures a small child has already felt.

## Why the structure matters for learning

Each verse follows the same shape: name the sense, give two or three examples, then move to the next one. That consistency is deliberate. Predictable structure lets a young learner focus on the new vocabulary in each verse instead of also having to figure out a new pattern every time. By the final chorus, the song has cycled through all five senses twice, once in the opening summary and once in detail, which is the kind of repetition that helps new words stick after just one or two listens.
