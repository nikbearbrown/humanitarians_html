Volunteer developer roles are usually vague about what a day actually looks like. Mycroft is specific about it: pair up with one other developer, pick one clearly scoped AI agent, and take it from concept to a production-ready tool. This video walks through exactly what that path looks like for anyone considering joining.

## What Mycroft is

Mycroft is described as an open-source educational experiment that uses AI to invest in AI. It's a volunteer opportunity that's eligible for OPT, and it's built as a hands-on laboratory rather than a traditional internship, meaning the goal is to discover what approaches actually work in agentic AI systems by building and testing them directly, not by reading about them. Developers join a collaborative community building an ecosystem of specialized AI agents that work together to analyze the fast-moving artificial intelligence sector.

## The core structure: pairs, one tool, real scope

Every developer partners with one other person, and the pair focuses on building a single, high-impact tool within the Mycroft framework, essentially their own specialized agent with a clear, narrow purpose. That constraint is deliberate. Rather than spreading effort across a huge framework, each team's mission is focused: take one tool from concept to production-ready status, then either refine it further or move on to another component in the growing ecosystem.

## Examples of what a team might build

The kinds of agents described give a concrete sense of scope. A financial data agent might pull metrics from a ticker symbol and generate a comprehensive report. A social media monitoring agent might track sentiment and emerging trends for specific companies. An insider trading analysis tool might identify patterns in executive stock movements. A congressional trade monitor might spot potential market-moving legislation by watching how lawmakers trade. These are meant as illustrations of the range available, not a fixed menu, and developers have real freedom to pursue what interests them most within that space.

## The orchestration layer, for those who want systems work

Not every developer wants to build a single-purpose agent. For those more interested in system architecture, there's the option to work on the Mycroft orchestration layer itself, the coordination engine that resolves conflicts between agents, prioritizes actions, and keeps the overall system coherent as more specialized tools get added. This is framed as where a different kind of engineering challenge lives: designing the mechanisms that let dozens or eventually hundreds of independent agents work together without stepping on each other.

## What developers actually gain

Beyond the immediate deliverable, the experience is framed around a specific set of skills: designing and implementing agentic AI systems, building tools that process complex financial information, and creating coordination mechanisms between autonomous agents, all aimed at deploying production-ready AI solutions with real-world applications. It's described as more than a technical challenge, an ongoing, collaborative experiment where the team builds in order to learn what actually works in AI-powered investment intelligence, rather than assuming it in advance.

This project sits within the Mycroft Financial AI track of the Humanitarians AI Fellows program, and it's aimed at developers, whether students or professionals, who want hands-on, OPT-eligible experience building real agentic AI systems rather than working through a purely theoretical curriculum.
