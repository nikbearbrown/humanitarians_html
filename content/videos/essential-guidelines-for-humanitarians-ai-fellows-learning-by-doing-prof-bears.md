Professor Bear records this video as a direct answer to the volume of individual emails he gets asking how to join Humanitarians AI as a fellow. Rather than explaining it one person at a time, he lays out exactly how the volunteer agreement works, who to contact, and what is expected once someone is in the program.

## Contact HR, not Professor Bear

The first and most repeated instruction in the video is to email HR at Humanitarians AI directly rather than reaching out to Professor Bear personally. He notes he receives roughly 20 emails a day from people asking for a volunteer agreement, and he is not the person who handles that process. Before emailing anyone, he directs prospective fellows to the Humanitarians AI website, the fellows program page, the GitHub for active projects, and two specific YouTube playlists: one aimed at fellows (covering practical skills like editing video with Adobe Premiere Rush, writing a YouTube title, and cleaning up audio) and one showing real examples of weekly reports, such as a fellow's Colab notebook walking through data bias detection.

## The program runs on OPT, and 20 hours a week is a legal requirement

Humanitarians AI structures its volunteer agreements to satisfy Optional Practical Training (OPT) requirements for international students, which means fellows commit to 20 hours of STEM-related work per week by law, not by preference. Professor Bear is explicit that questions about visa status, OPT eligibility, and whether a given arrangement satisfies a school's requirements go to the fellow's own school office, not to him or to HR. Humanitarians AI does not answer immigration questions.

## Weekly video reports are the deliverable

Fellows are expected to produce roughly 5 to 10 minutes of high-quality video a week explaining their work, backed by a corresponding write-up or notebook on GitHub so someone else can run the code and learn from it directly. The program's philosophy, in Professor Bear's words, is "learning by doing": a report typically opens with a short discussion of the underlying theory, then walks through the actual work, whether that's detecting bias in a dataset or something else. He notes the videos are intentionally rawer than a polished tutorial channel; struggling with a specific problem, such as not being able to afford an API key and working around it, is exactly the kind of content that belongs in a report.

## At-will, one month at a time, with straightforward renewal

The initial volunteer agreement runs one month, with possible renewal in roughly two-month increments based on performance. Unlike a volunteer contract that might bind someone to an organization for six months, Humanitarians AI's agreement is at-will in both directions: the fellow can leave at any time, and the organization can end the agreement at any time. Professor Bear frames this as intentional. The goal is for fellows to move on to paid jobs, and he says the organization wants to help people get there rather than hold them to a long commitment. Renewal is described as easy if weekly reports have been filed consistently, since that data covers most of what's needed. Fellows are asked to request renewal at least a week ahead of an expiring agreement rather than asking for a same-day exception.

## What counts as success, and what doesn't

The program does not grade fellows the way a class would. The only real way to fail, per Professor Bear, is to not participate: making a good-faith effort to learn and produce weekly reports is what keeps an agreement in good standing, not perfection. He also addresses a related, common request: he will not write recommendation letters for people he has not directly worked with, and encourages fellows to seek those letters from the mentors or professors they actually collaborated with.

## Key takeaways

- Email HR at Humanitarians AI for volunteer agreement questions; do not email Professor Bear directly.
- The agreement satisfies OPT, which legally requires 20 hours of STEM-related work per week.
- Weekly deliverables are 5 to 10 minutes of video plus a backing write-up or notebook on GitHub.
- Agreements are at-will and typically start at one month, renewing in roughly two-month increments.
- Immigration and visa questions go to your school's international office, not to Humanitarians AI.
