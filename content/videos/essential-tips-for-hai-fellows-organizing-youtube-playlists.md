A good video is only useful if the people who need it can actually find it. That is the entire premise behind this short onboarding note from Professor Bear to Humanitarians AI Fellows, prompted by a genuinely nice video the media team put together on Adobe Rush that nobody could locate afterward, because it had not been added to a single playlist.

## The problem with an unplaced video

Someone on the media team produced a solid walkthrough of Adobe Rush, a video editing tool, but published it without placing it into any playlist. That single missing step meant the video effectively disappeared for anyone who wasn't already watching the channel closely. Professor Bear frames this as a recurring issue worth calling out directly: the content itself can be excellent, but if it isn't organized, it fails at its actual job.

## Two obvious audiences, two obvious playlists

The Adobe Rush video has two natural audiences at Humanitarians AI. First, the volunteers who are nearly all making videos themselves and need to learn editing basics, which points to the HAI Fellows playlist. Second, anyone curious about the tool itself regardless of role, which points to a more general "nerd stuff" playlist. Professor Bear's guidance is simple: when a video clearly serves an identifiable audience, put it where that audience will look.

## Why the HAI Fellows playlist matters most

The HAI Fellows playlist gets singled out as the one that matters most, because it is the shared reference point new and existing fellows are expected to check before asking a question that has already been answered on video. Without consistent playlist placement, the same basic explanations end up getting repeated over and over, which wastes time for everyone involved. A well-maintained playlist turns one good explanation into a resource the whole team can reuse indefinitely.

## Adobe Rush as a practical starting point

Beyond the organizational lesson, the video also flags Adobe Rush itself as a genuinely useful recommendation: it is free for Northeastern students and offers a free version for anyone else. For fellows who are new to making videos, this is presented as a low-barrier way to start editing without needing a paid subscription or specialized equipment.

## Key takeaways

- A video's usefulness depends on where it gets placed, not just on how good the video itself is.
- New videos should go into every playlist that matches their actual audience, not just one.
- The HAI Fellows playlist is the shared reference point meant to prevent repeating the same basic explanations.
- Adobe Rush is a free (or free-tier) editing tool worth trying for fellows just getting started with video.
