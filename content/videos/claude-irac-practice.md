Does practicing IRAC with Claude mean Claude hands you the finished answer? No. It walks you through the steps in a fixed order, and it will not skip ahead to the end. The irac-practice skill is built specifically to prevent that shortcut.

## What a skill is here

The irac-practice skill is a folder Claude reads before it acts. One file, SKILL.md, holds the whole instruction set for drilling IRAC in plain language, with no hidden logic. Claude reads it, then acts on it; the file is the program defining exactly how the drill runs.

## A linear drill: pose, pause, check

The drill lives in a step section, and Claude reads each step in order: pose the hypothetical, then wait for your Issue, then your Rule, then your Application, in that exact order, before it looks at your Conclusion at all. This is linear, with no skipping ahead unless a step explicitly says otherwise. That ordering is the whole point of the drill. IRAC, Issue, Rule, Application, Conclusion, is a method for legal analysis, and the skill enforces going through the method rather than jumping to the answer.

## The check that actually matters

One check inside the skill is specific and worth naming directly: stating the right Conclusion without showing the Application earns nothing. The Application is where the Rule actually meets the facts, worked through one fact at a time. A Conclusion that skips that step is a guess wearing the right answer, not evidence that the reasoning behind it was sound.

## Claude checks the process, not just the outcome

Claude is not grading your Conclusion in isolation. It is checking whether you showed the Application, one fact at a time, on the way to it. That is a deliberate design choice: a student who has memorized or guessed the correct outcome for a given hypothetical, without being able to show how the rule applies to the specific facts, has not demonstrated the skill IRAC is meant to build. The drill exists to catch exactly that gap.

## Running the drill yourself

The practical exercise is to give Claude a short fact pattern, one paragraph, and ask it to read the irac-practice skill without giving you the answer. It should ask for your Issue first, then your Rule, then your Application, and only check your Conclusion after all three have been given. That sequencing forces the same discipline the skill is built around: reasoning shown before an answer is accepted.

## Why the order is the point

The value of this drill is not in producing a correct final answer faster. It is in forcing the intermediate steps to be shown and checked before the final answer is even considered. A student who can only produce the Conclusion, without walking through how the Rule meets the facts, has not actually practiced the skill the drill is meant to build. Enforcing the order is what turns this into practice rather than a shortcut to the answer.
