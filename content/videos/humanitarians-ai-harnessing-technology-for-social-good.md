Most conversations about artificial intelligence focus on what the technology can do. Fewer ask who it is actually built for. Humanitarians AI Incorporated, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit, was founded to answer that second question directly, positioning itself as an organization that builds AI for underserved communities rather than around them.

## A mission built on four programs

Rather than treating "AI for good" as a slogan, Humanitarians AI structures its work around four distinct programs, each targeting a different way technology can serve people. The Fellows Program mentors emerging researchers, turning newcomers who arrive with questions into contributors equipped to build fair, transparent systems. AI for Good takes that same commitment to fairness and applies it directly to software, tackling real-world inequities with algorithms designed around accountability rather than function alone. Lyrical Literacy uses music and AI together to support brain health, language acquisition, and cognitive development. And Botspeak teaches people, from complete novices to experienced practitioners, how to communicate with large language models effectively and ethically.

## Mentorship as the throughline

What connects these four programs is not a shared technology stack but a shared method: mentorship. The Fellows Program frames its researchers as people who "come to us with questions and leave with tools," a description that treats capability-building as the actual product, not a side effect of shipping software. That same mentorship thread runs through Lyrical Literacy, where researchers are described as "remixing learning" by building song books and apps, and through Botspeak, which runs workshops and videos aimed at democratizing access to AI rather than gatekeeping it behind technical jargon.

## Code as compassion, not just function

The AI for Good program frames its guiding question plainly: for every tool built, who does it serve, and how? That question reframes software development as an ethical exercise as much as a technical one. Fairness, transparency, and accountability are treated as design constraints from the start, not compliance checkboxes added after a product ships. The description of writing "software for justice, not just function" captures the distinction the organization is drawing between building something that works and building something that serves the people it claims to help.

## Music, language, and access

Lyrical Literacy stands out as the most unusual of the four programs, treating music as a legitimate tool for cognitive development rather than a lifestyle add-on. By pairing neuroscience with song, the program aims to strengthen brain health and language acquisition, treating "music as medicine" in a literal, applied sense. Botspeak, by contrast, addresses a much more immediate skill gap: most people have never been taught how to prompt an AI system well, and Botspeak exists to close that gap through direct instruction rather than assuming fluency will develop on its own.
