Most volunteer tech projects hand you a ticket and a codebase you didn't design. Madison works differently: it's an open-source agentic framework where developers build their own specialized AI agent, in a two-person team, with a clear and specific purpose, and the whole thing is structured to be OPT-eligible for international students who need that kind of volunteer work to count.

## What Madison actually is

Madison is a collaborative, open-source ecosystem of specialized AI agents that work together to create, optimize, and measure marketing strategies. Rather than one monolithic system, it's built as a set of individually purposeful agents, each handling one specific job well, coordinated so the results support a coherent marketing and branding effort. The framework is explicitly described as a hands-on laboratory: the point isn't to follow a prescribed path, it's to discover what actually works in practice by building and testing real tools.

## How teams are structured

Developers join in pairs and focus on building one specific, high-impact tool within the framework, essentially their own specialized agent with a clear purpose. The project lists several concrete examples of what that can look like: a sentiment analysis agent that tracks brand perception across social media and generates reports, a content generation agent that produces multi-channel marketing materials while maintaining a consistent brand voice, customer journey tools that map and optimize the experience from first impression through to a loyal customer, and multi-armed bandit systems that continuously optimize which content performs best.

## Visual and orchestration work

Not every contribution is agent-level. Some developers focus on visual concept agents that generate AI-assisted design assets for campaigns, while others work on the Madison orchestration layer itself, the coordination engine responsible for brand consistency across everything the individual agents produce. That orchestration work involves resolving conflicts between agents, prioritizing actions, and keeping the overall marketing effort strategically aligned, which is a meaningfully different kind of engineering challenge than building a single-purpose agent.

## The mission: concept to production

The scope for each pair is intentionally focused rather than open-ended: take one tool from concept to production-ready status. Once an agent is deployed, a developer can choose to refine it further or move on to build another component in the growing ecosystem. That constraint matters, because with hundreds of potential marketing agents that could plausibly fit into the framework, the project needs contributors who ship something real rather than starting many things and finishing none.

## What developers actually gain

Beyond the marketing outcomes, the project is framed explicitly as an educational one: developers gain experience designing and implementing agentic AI systems, building tools that process complex consumer and brand information, constructing coordination mechanisms between autonomous agents, and deploying production-ready AI solutions with real-world applications. It's positioned as building to learn, with the collaborative structure meant to surface which approaches genuinely work in AI-powered marketing.

## Who this is for

This is aimed at developers, particularly international students on OPT, who want hands-on experience building agentic AI systems in a collaborative, open-source setting rather than working through a solo tutorial. It's a project under Humanitarians AI, and joining means partnering with another developer to ship a real, deployed agent as part of the Madison ecosystem.
