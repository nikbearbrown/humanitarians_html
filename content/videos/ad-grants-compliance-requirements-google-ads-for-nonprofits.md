A $10,000 monthly Google Ad Grant is a genuinely large amount of free advertising for a nonprofit, but it comes with a compliance checklist that will get an account suspended if ignored. Professor Bear, drawing on real experience managing the grant for Humanitarians AI, walks through that checklist in detail, built from direct experience rather than an official Google source, with the explicit caveat that this reflects the team's best understanding rather than a statement on Google's behalf.

## Campaigns and ad groups as themes

The structural foundation is at least one active campaign, where a campaign functions as a theme. Humanitarians AI's campaigns map onto its actual programs: Botspeak, an AI fluency framework; Lyrical Literacy, using music to train and exercise the brain; the Fellows program, extending learning after graduation; and AI for Good, where volunteers build software for other nonprofits. Every campaign needs at least two ad groups underneath it. Using Lyrical Literacy as the worked example, one ad group covers songs framed as brain exercise, promoting videos already made for the project, while the second ad group covers teaching viewers how to make their own specialized songs for their own kids. Two campaigns' worth of content becomes one campaign, Lyrical Literacy, with two distinct ad groups sitting underneath it.

## Sitelinks, conversion tracking, and bidding

Each campaign needs sitelinks pointing to specific landing pages, and those pages need conversion tracking attached so the team can confirm visitors are actually arriving from the ad and following through. These landing pages are deliberately built as internal pages not easily reachable through normal site navigation, which is how the team can be confident traffic arriving there came specifically from the ad. Bidding strategy and geotargeting round out the structural requirements: Humanitarians AI uses a conversion-based bidding strategy, and restricts geotargeting primarily to English-speaking countries, and to the United States specifically for simplicity, even though the underlying YouTube content could theoretically reach a global audience.

## Keyword quality: no single generic words

Keyword quality gets its own emphasis because it's where the strictest scrutiny applies. Single, generic words like "music" are explicitly disallowed, since a word that broad brings in visitors with no real connection to the nonprofit's actual mission, someone searching for a completely unrelated topic who happens to land on the site by accident. The requirement instead is specific, mission-relevant keyword phrases: brain exercise songs for kids, language learning for kids, educational songs for children. These longer, more specific phrases are what's known as long-tail keywords. Any single long-tail keyword draws fewer total searches, but the people who do search it are actually interested in that specific thing, which is exactly the kind of visitor Google wants an Ad Grant account sending traffic to, and exactly the kind of visitor the nonprofit wants arriving on its own site.

## Website compliance: ownership, relevance, and transparency

The website itself carries its own set of requirements. The nonprofit must own its own domain, and running ads from an ad network on that same site is a firm violation. Every page a visitor lands on needs to be relevant to the nonprofit's actual mission: a page about music and brain training should never lead a visitor to unrelated content. Humanitarians AI's site keeps identifying details, including its EIN, visible in the footer on every page so visitors can independently verify it's a legitimate nonprofit through standard lookup tools, alongside a privacy policy, a cookie policy, terms of service, and an Ad Grants compliance page, all built as shared components so they appear consistently across every page of the site rather than needing to be added individually. No broken links is part of the same standard, something the team has been checking by hand and is now building tooling to check automatically instead. The site also needs to avoid making commercial claims and stay transparent about how donated funds are actually used.

## Trust through external fundraising tools

On the donation side, Professor Bear recommends routing funds through established external platforms like PayPal Giving and GoFundMe rather than building a custom donation flow from scratch, specifically because of the trust those platforms carry. Both PayPal and GoFundMe independently verify an organization's nonprofit status before enabling giving through their tools, which gives a donor a second, independent layer of confidence beyond the nonprofit's own claims about itself, something a smaller or newer organization especially benefits from.

## Key takeaways

- Every campaign needs at least one active campaign with a minimum of two ad groups underneath it, each representing a distinct angle on the same theme.
- Sitelinks need dedicated landing pages with conversion tracking attached, plus a defined bidding strategy and geotargeting scoped to the actual audience.
- Single generic keywords are disallowed; specific, mission-relevant long-tail keyword phrases are what Google Ad Grants requires and rewards.
- Website compliance requires owning your own domain, running no on-site ad network, keeping every page mission-relevant, and displaying identifying and legal information (EIN, privacy policy, cookie policy, terms of service) on every page.
- Routing donations through established platforms like PayPal Giving and GoFundMe adds independent, third-party verification of nonprofit status that builds donor trust.
