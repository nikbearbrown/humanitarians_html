Everyone knows The Wizard of Oz from Dorothy's side of the story, but what did the journey down the yellow brick road look like from four paws off the ground? This lyrical retelling flips the perspective entirely, putting Toto at the center of the adventure and turning a familiar tale into a reggae-rhythmed poem about loyalty and courage.

## Retelling Oz from Toto's point of view

The piece opens mid-crisis: a house falling from the sky, a witch's scream, dust everywhere, and Dorothy holding on tight while Toto tries to make sense of the "yellow rock" beneath them. From there, the story tracks the familiar beats of Oz, the singing trees, the lion, the straw man, the tin man, but narrates all of it through Toto's eyes, with the dog's fear, loyalty, and determination driving the emotional core rather than Dorothy's.

## Rhythm and poetry as the storytelling engine

Instead of straight narration, the story is delivered as rhythmic spoken word set to a reggae-inspired beat, with recurring lines like "you stayed by me, you always do" anchoring the emotional throughline. That repetition and rhyme isn't just stylistic; it's built to reinforce vocabulary and story recall, which is the whole idea behind pairing poetic phrasing with music for younger listeners and reluctant readers alike.

## Loyalty over magic

The emotional turn of the story is Toto's role in the famous curtain scene: it's Toto, not Dorothy, who tugs back the curtain and exposes the wizard as a lie. From there the story lands its real message, that "no lion's badge, no wizard's game" was ever the point. What actually got Dorothy home was loyalty and love, not magic or metal scrolls. The closing verses make that explicit: Toto insists that if you think of him as just a pet, "you haven't heard my journey yet."
