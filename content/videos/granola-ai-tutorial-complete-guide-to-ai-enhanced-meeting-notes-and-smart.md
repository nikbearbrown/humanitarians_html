Sitting in back-to-back meetings usually means choosing between paying attention and taking usable notes. Granola AI is built to remove that trade-off by combining a person's own manual notes with a real-time transcript, then using AI to turn the two together into an organized, actionable summary once the meeting ends.

## Getting started with Granola

Granola is a downloadable app for Mac or PC, found by searching for it directly and downloading it from its website. Once installed, it presents a simple interface: whenever a meeting starts, Granola prompts the user to record it and begin taking notes alongside the audio. The core idea is that you're never just transcribing or just note-taking, you're doing both at once, with the app filling in the gaps afterward.

## Real-time transcription meets manual notes

During a live walkthrough, a recorded meeting is played back while Granola captures the transcript in the background. The app doesn't just log what was said, it lets the user keep typing their own shorthand notes at the same time, then reconciles the two into a fuller record once the recording finishes. After the meeting, generating the notes produces a structured breakdown: meeting context, the current progress being reported, next steps, blockers, and follow-up items, all pulled from what was actually discussed.

## Turning a recording into a structured summary

Once notes are generated, Granola doesn't stop at a transcript dump. It uses GPT-4 to enhance the raw notes into a proper summary, and the app can also list out action items directly, draft a follow-up email based on what was discussed, or compile the questions and answers that came up during the meeting. In one example, a note-taker who mentioned working on training a GAN model got back a specific summary of next steps: continue training the model and complete the inference runs, pulled straight from what was said out loud rather than typed manually. That's the practical payoff of pairing transcription with generation: someone who steps away from a meeting, or wasn't even present, can still see exactly what needs to happen next.

## Templates for different meeting types

Granola supports switching between different note templates depending on what the meeting actually needs. Options include capturing background and pain points, budget or timeline details when they come up, an existing product demo, or a general topic summary. Choosing the right template changes what the AI pulls out and prioritizes, so a sales demo and a team stand-up don't get forced into the same generic format.

## Sharing notes and generating follow-ups

Once notes exist, sharing them is built into the workflow. Users can copy a shareable link, copy the note text directly, send it by email, or post it to Slack, which matters given how many organizations already run their day-to-day communication through Slack. The same GPT-4 layer that generates summaries can also draft the follow-up email itself, so the step of writing "here's what we discussed and what's next" doesn't have to start from a blank page.

## Works across every meeting platform

Granola isn't tied to one video tool. It works across Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams, and can also be used to record standalone calls, including something as simple as a WhatsApp call, by starting a new note and switching on recording. It also supports multiple accounts, so a work account's notes stay separate from personal ones, with additional hub and settings options for managing the workspace. The tool is free to start, with more advanced features requiring an upgrade.

## Key takeaways

- Granola pairs manual note-taking with real-time transcription rather than replacing one with the other.
