What would The Wizard of Oz sound like if the story were narrated not by Dorothy, but by the small dog who never left her side? This piece reimagines the classic tale entirely from Toto's point of view, told in rhythmic, reggae-inflected verse that turns a familiar adventure into a story about loyalty, courage, and staying close when everything else is uncertain.

## Retelling a classic through a different narrator

Shifting the narrator from Dorothy to Toto changes what the story is actually about. Toto describes the tornado that "crashed Dorothy flat," the witch's scream, and the cloud of dust, but the emotional center is Dorothy holding him close, "heart full of shock," while he scans the strange, singing landscape of Oz. From there, the familiar characters appear filtered through a dog's-eye perspective: a lion who "shook like leaves with something to prove," a straw man who "lost his stuff in every stride," and a tin man so stiff he'd "freeze up solid if left alone." None of these are new characters, but seeing them through Toto's eyes reframes the entire journey as something he is protecting Dorothy through, not just witnessing.

## Loyalty as the emotional throughline

The chorus keeps returning to a single idea: "you stayed by me, you always do." That refrain does the narrative work of tying together every set piece in the story, from the poppy fields to the witch's fire, under one theme of steady loyalty. When Toto describes tugging the curtain to reveal the wizard's true face, the moment isn't framed as a clever trick, it's framed as an act of protection: "I could see it was a lie... I tug the curtain, showed his face." The wizard's exposure becomes proof that Toto's instinct to protect Dorothy was right all along, not just a plot twist borrowed from the original story.

## Why the perspective and the rhythm matter for learning

This piece is part of the Lyrical Literacy program, which uses AI-generated music to build personalized educational songs. The pairing of poetic retelling with a reggae-inspired rhythm isn't just a stylistic choice, it's a structural one: rhythmic, rhyming language is easier to recall and repeat than plain prose, which supports listening comprehension and vocabulary building through repetition. Retelling a familiar story from an unfamiliar narrator's perspective also gives listeners practice with a specific reading skill, recognizing that the same events can carry different meaning depending on whose eyes you see them through, which builds character empathy alongside the language benefits.

## The ending reframes the whole story

By the final verses, Toto explicitly rejects the idea that the story's magic came from Oz's trappings: "No need for metal scrolls or fame. No lion's badge. No wizard's game. Just her soft voice, her hand in mine." That's the piece's real argument, that the story's protective, loyal bond between a girl and her dog was the actual source of courage the whole time, not the wizard, the yellow brick road, or any of the fantastical elements layered on top of it.
