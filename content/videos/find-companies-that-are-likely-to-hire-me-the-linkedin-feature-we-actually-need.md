LinkedIn has more data about who actually gets hired where than almost any platform on earth, and it's mostly sitting unused. This video is a pointed rant about that gap, and a concrete proposal for the feature that would fix it: a search filter called "Find Companies That Are Likely to Hire Me."

## The problem with LinkedIn as it stands

The frustration driving this video is specific: LinkedIn, as a job search tool, mostly functions as a stream of spam messages from people who don't know who the recipient is or what they do. For a platform sitting on this much information about careers, hiring, and company behavior, that's a genuinely wasted opportunity. The core critique isn't that LinkedIn lacks data. It's that the platform isn't using the data it already has to do the one thing job seekers actually need help with: finding companies that will realistically hire them.

## What the feature would actually do

The proposed feature is a search filter, potentially a premium one, that narrows a job search down to companies that have a track record of hiring people with a background similar to yours. Not similar in some vague sense, but similar in the specific ways that matter to hiring: whether you're an international student, a recent graduate, a mid-career professional making a transition, someone with a non-traditional background, or someone coming out of retirement. The filter would surface companies that have actually hired people fitting that profile before, rather than leaving a job seeker to guess or apply blind.

## Why LinkedIn already has the data to build this

The reason this isn't a far-fetched request is that LinkedIn already collects nearly everything the feature would need. Employee background information, career progression patterns, whether a hire came from overseas or domestically, education and experience history, remote work patterns, and company-level hiring trends are all things LinkedIn profiles and company pages already surface in fragments. A worked example in the video looks at one person's profile: someone who got an undergraduate degree overseas and a master's degree in the US, likely on a visa path, working at a specific company. That single profile is a data point LinkedIn could use to help someone with a similar background find companies with a comparable hiring pattern. Multiply that by every profile on the platform and the underlying signal is already there, just not surfaced as a search filter.

## Who this would actually help

The value of a feature like this isn't abstract. It's aimed squarely at the people who most need to filter out wasted effort: international students who need visa sponsorship and can't afford to apply to companies that never sponsor, recent graduates trying to land a first job, mid-career professionals attempting a transition into a new field, people with non-traditional career paths, and simply anyone tired of sending applications into companies that, based on their own hiring history, were never going to consider them. Framed as a premium feature, it would also give LinkedIn Premium subscribers something that actually justifies the subscription cost, rather than the current experience of mostly filtering an inbox full of cold pitches.
