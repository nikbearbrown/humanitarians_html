Companies are no longer just hiring for a list of skills. Increasingly, they are hiring for how well a person's skills complement an AI system, how well someone can "dance with a machine." That shift raises an obvious problem: most people have no structured way to think about what it actually means to work well with AI. Botspeak is an attempt to answer that with a concrete framework rather than vague advice.

## Nine pillars built to outlast the current AI moment

Botspeak organizes AI fluency into nine pillars, each chosen because it stays relevant even as specific tools and models change. Strategic delegation is about knowing which parts of a task to hand to AI and which to keep for yourself. Effective communication is about writing prompts with real clarity and intent, not vague requests. Critical evaluation means never simply trusting an AI's output and instead systematically checking it. Technical understanding covers applying the right prompt patterns for the situation at hand, and ethical reasoning centers on accountability and privacy, knowing which lines never to cross.

The framework continues with stochastic reasoning, which is about understanding that AI output is fundamentally probabilistic rather than deterministic, so working with it well means trusting a distribution of outcomes rather than expecting a single right answer every time. Learning by doing emphasizes building real intuition through practice rather than theory alone, and rapid prototyping is about using quick iterative cycles to accelerate ideas. The ninth pillar, theoretical foundations, asks a more philosophical question: what can AI actually know, and what can it never understand? That question is described as rooted in philosophical skepticism, a deliberate choice to keep the framework honest about AI's limits rather than treating it as a black box that always has the right answer.

## Three modes of interaction

Beyond the nine pillars, Botspeak names three fundamental modes for how a person and an AI system can relate to each other. In automation mode, the AI simply obeys, carrying out a defined task without back-and-forth collaboration. In augmentation mode, the human and the AI act as co-thinkers, working through a problem together rather than one directing the other. In agency mode, the AI moves on its own, but within a scope the human has already mapped out. Knowing which mode a given task calls for, and deliberately choosing it, is itself part of AI fluency.

## Why the framework matters now

The core argument behind Botspeak is that the challenge ahead is not really about AI capability at all. It is about learning to think together with AI systems, treating that skill as its own kind of literacy. As AI becomes embedded in more of how work gets done, the pillars and modes in this framework give people a shared vocabulary for talking about collaboration with AI, rather than treating every interaction as a one-off experiment.

## Key takeaways

- Botspeak is a nine-pillar framework for AI fluency, covering delegation, communication, evaluation, technical understanding, ethics, probabilistic reasoning, hands-on learning, prototyping, and theoretical limits.
- The framework is rooted in philosophical skepticism, deliberately asking what AI can and cannot truly know.
- Three modes of interaction, automation, augmentation, and agency, describe the different ways a person can relate to an AI system on a given task.
- The framework is built to remain useful as AI technology itself continues to evolve, since it focuses on the human side of the collaboration.
