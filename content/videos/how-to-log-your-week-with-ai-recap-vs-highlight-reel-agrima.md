Agrima records a short, practical demonstration of how she uses Claude to write her own weekly work log, and why she deliberately avoids the "highlight reel" format most updates default to. The video walks through two versions of the same prompt, showing how a small structural change produces a much more honest report.

## The problem with a flat list

Most weekly updates dress up whatever happened that week into a set of wins with equal weight. Agrima wanted something closer to an actual log: what got done, kept clearly separate from what's just getting started. Her first prompt to Claude asked for something simple, a script that just logs the week with no ranking and no spin: a nonprofit marketing article, the video that goes with it, and an upcoming talk at Suffolk University. Claude returned exactly that, a flat list of three entries with no sense of what was finished and what hadn't started yet.

## Why a flat list is misleading

The flat list treats "I published this" the same as "I'm speaking on Wednesday." Both read as accomplishments, even though one is complete and the other hasn't happened. Agrima identifies this as the core issue with typical highlight-style updates: by giving every item the same weight, they blur the line between what actually shipped and what's still just a plan.

## Revising the prompt to split done from next

She revised the prompt to explicitly split the same three items into two categories: Done and Next. Re-running it with that instruction changed the shape of the report completely. Under Done: the article was published, and the video came out in both formats. Under Next: the Suffolk University talk, with Yatra, about Humanitarians AI, still ahead of her. Nothing under Next is finished yet, and Agrima's point is that saying so explicitly is what makes the log honest rather than aspirational.

## The resulting weekly log

The final version reads as a real log rather than a highlight reel: one article shipped, one video actually produced in both formats, and one talk still ahead. Agrima frames the Done/Next split as a small habit with an outsized effect, since it's the difference between a recap that reflects reality and one that quietly inflates unfinished work into accomplishments.

## Applying the same prompt to your own week

Agrima's closing suggestion is to reuse the same two-step approach: ask Claude for a flat log of your week first, then revise it to split Done from Next. The habit isn't about the tool, it's about the structure, keeping yourself honest about what actually happened versus what you just started thinking about.

## Key takeaways

- A flat weekly list treats finished work and upcoming plans as equally significant, which misrepresents progress.
- Splitting a log into Done and Next categories forces clarity about what's actually complete.
- The same prompt, revised once, produces a meaningfully more honest report from the same three facts.
- This is a repeatable habit, not a one-off script: reuse the same two-step prompt each week.
- The goal is accountability to yourself, not a polished summary for an audience.
