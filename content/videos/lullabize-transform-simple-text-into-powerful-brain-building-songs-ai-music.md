Turning a block of plain text into a singable, memorable song used to require actual musical training. Professor Bear's walkthrough of Lullabize, a custom GPT built for the Lyrical Literacy Project, is a demonstration of skipping that requirement entirely, built on research into what makes simple songs genuinely useful for the brain rather than just pleasant to listen to.

## The research behind the tool

Lullabize wasn't built as a novelty. It's grounded in research on best practices for using simple songs in cognitive development, resting on the idea that singing enriches areas of the brain that logic-based learning doesn't reach on its own. That's the premise behind the Lyrical Literacy Project more broadly: getting people, kids especially but older adults too, to engage with simple songs as a form of active brain exercise, since exercising the brain matters more, not less, as people get older.

## How the custom GPT works

To make that research usable, the team packaged it into a custom GPT that anyone can open directly. The starting prompt is intentionally simple: it's literally text pulled straight from Mother Goose on Project Gutenberg, along with a small set of commands the GPT will list out for you. The mechanics are equally simple from the user's side: paste in some text, and the GPT applies a set of rules built from that cognitive-development research to turn it into song lyrics, complete with a suggested musical style and meta tags for delivery choices like whispering. Every rule the GPT applies is visible and editable, so anyone who wants to build their own version with adjusted rules, longer or shorter songs, different structural choices, can see exactly what to change. For a personalized song, Professor Bear notes you'd typically fold in details about the actual child or person the song is for: their name, things they like, whether that's Roblox or soccer or something else entirely.

## Rendering the lyrics in Suno

Generating lyrics is only half the process; the other half is turning that text into an actual sung recording, and for that the video walks through Suno. For the first example, a reimagined take on "Pat-a-Cake," Professor Bear uploaded his own voice to Suno and used it as the vocal basis for the render. Suno's voice upload feature keeps the output within the uploaded voice's actual range, which matters if the point is to sing along yourself: matching your own natural range makes a song far easier to follow than trying to match an unfamiliar performer's voice.

## Two takes, two different tones

The first render came out in a style Professor Bear describes as rough and gruff, which he acknowledges naturally reflects his own voice and taste, but recognizes probably isn't the ideal tone for a song meant for young kids. That realization led directly to a second example: a song about honeybees, deliberately built to be mellower and more chill, still using an uploaded voice but one recorded in a calmer, less aggressive style. The lesson he draws out explicitly is that if you're making a song for your own kid, using your voice or your kid's voice makes it more personal, and it's worth being deliberate about tone rather than defaulting to whatever style comes most naturally to you.

## The bigger point: make it personal

Across both examples, the throughline is that Lullabize and Suno together are meant as tools for other people to use themselves, not primarily as a showcase of finished songs from Humanitarians AI. Professor Bear is direct about this: a song is likely to land better with a kid, a friend, or a partner if that person made it, or had it made specifically for them, rather than receiving something generic produced by someone else. The goal of the series is teaching the process itself, prompts included, so viewers can replicate it with their own text and their own voice.

## Key takeaways

- Lullabize is a custom GPT built on cognitive-development research into why simple songs help brain development, especially in children and older adults.
- The GPT works from a simple starting prompt, in this demo drawn from Mother Goose text, applying a visible, editable rule set to generate lyrics and a suggested style.
- Suno renders the lyrics into an actual song, and uploading your own voice keeps the result within your natural vocal range for easier singalongs.

## Try it yourself

Lullabize is available directly through Humanitarians AI's Lyrical Literacy Project for anyone who wants to try turning their own text into a song. Start with a short, simple passage, whether that's a nursery rhyme or something personal, run it through the GPT's rule set, and render the result in Suno using your own voice or a family member's for a song that actually feels made for the person it's meant for.
