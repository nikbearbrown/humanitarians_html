Most competition announcements land in an inbox and get skipped. This one is worth a second look, because it is free to enter, open to students and recent graduates, and built around the kind of hands-on work that actually teaches something, regardless of whether a team walks away with a prize.

## What the competition actually is

The AgentX-AgentBeats Competition is hosted by Berkeley RDI, an AI group at UC Berkeley, and it builds on the Agentic AI MOOC community of more than 32,000 learners. It brings together builders, researchers, engineers, and AI enthusiasts from around the world with one goal: push the boundaries of agentic AI through real benchmarking and real agent-building work, not just a lecture series.

## Two phases, one shared goal

The competition runs in two phases. Phase 1 asks participants to build or enhance benchmarks for agentic AI, the tests that measure how well an AI agent actually performs. Phase 2 asks participants to develop AI agents that excel on those benchmarks. Together, the two phases are meant to create high-quality, broad-coverage, realistic agent evaluations as shared public goods, building toward a community-driven ecosystem of agent evaluation benchmarks that are standardized, reproducible, and discoverable rather than scattered and one-off.

## Why it is worth doing even without a prize

The case for joining is simple: doing is how you learn, more so than sitting through a class. The value of a project like this comes less from winning and more from the act of forming a team, building something real, and testing it against other people's work, the same kind of learning that comes from starting a company or shipping a real product rather than just completing coursework. With over $1 million in prizes, cloud credits, and API resources on the table, and more than 700 teams already signed up, there is also real upside if a team's work stands out. But the floor is what makes it worth doing: if a team puts in the time and does not win, they still walk away having learned a lot.

## Who it is realistically for

This is aimed squarely at students, recent graduates, and AI professionals who want real-world experience with agentic AI systems. It does not require deep credentials to start, just a willingness to form a team and commit the time. Since it appears to be free to sign up, the practical barrier to entry is low, and the learning opportunity from either phase, benchmark-building or agent-building, is substantial on its own.
