## Why tongue twisters work as brain training

The description behind the song lays out the reasoning in plain terms. Saying a tongue twister forces the brain to plan a sequence of similar-sounding syllables and then execute that plan through fast, precise mouth movements. Get the planning or the execution slightly wrong, and the phrase collapses into nonsense, which is immediate, low-stakes feedback. Repeated practice with that kind of feedback loop is what strengthens phonological awareness, the ability to hear and manipulate the sounds inside words, which in turn supports reading skills. The same repetition also exercises working memory, since a full tongue twister has to be held in mind while it is spoken, and it builds cognitive flexibility because the mouth has to shift quickly between similar but distinct sound patterns.
