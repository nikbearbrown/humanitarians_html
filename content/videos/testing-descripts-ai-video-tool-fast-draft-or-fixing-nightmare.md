Automatic video generation tools promise to turn a raw recording into a finished draft with almost no effort. This test puts that promise to the test with Descript's AI video feature, using a recording of the classic song "Over the River and Through the Woods."

## Setting up the test

The process starts simple: upload an audio recording, in this case a recorded performance of the holiday standard, and let Descript handle the rest automatically, including generating subtitles and assembling a video draft. On paper, this is exactly the kind of fast, AI-generated workflow that promises to save creators significant time on a first pass.

## What the tool actually delivers

The reality lands somewhere between impressive and frustrating. Descript does produce a rough video draft quickly, which is genuinely useful if all you need is something to visualize an idea or share with collaborators as a starting point. But the automatically generated subtitles are riddled with errors. Correcting them by hand would take roughly an hour, even for a song that only runs three to four minutes long. That's a significant time cost for a tool whose main selling point is speed.

## Where it's actually useful, and where it isn't

The honest verdict is that Descript's AI video feature has a narrower use case than its marketing suggests. It's a reasonable choice for storyboarding or getting a rough first draft together quickly, especially for creators who need to sketch out a concept fast or show a collaborator a rough cut without polishing anything first. But anyone expecting a ready-to-post, polished final video is going to be disappointed. The gap between the fast draft and a publishable result still requires real manual cleanup, and that gap is where the tool's promise starts to break down.
