Building a polished car commercial the traditional way takes real time. One creator spent 61 days modeling, lighting, and rendering a BMW ad in Unreal Engine, frame by frame, to get a result worthy of a car brand. The question this short video asks is simple: what happens if you hand that same challenge to AI video generation tools instead?

## The challenge

The project started after watching "I Spent 61 Days Making A Commercial For BMW In 3D," a video documenting the grind of traditional 3D animation: modeling the car, building the environment, tuning the lighting, and rendering it all out over two months of work. That timeline set the bar. Could a modern AI video tool like VEO 3 get anywhere close to that same commercial-grade result, and if so, how long would it actually take?

## From 10 minutes to 25

The plan going in was to treat this as a quick 10-minute experiment: type a few prompts, generate some clips, and see what came out. It did not stay that quick. Getting a result that felt close to a real BMW spot took more iteration than expected, and the project stretched to 25 minutes of actual work. That is still a dramatic contrast against 61 days of manual 3D production, but it is also an honest admission that "AI does it instantly" understates what it actually takes to get a usable result. Prompting, regenerating, and picking the right shot all take time, even when that time is measured in minutes rather than months.

## What the comparison actually shows

The value of a test like this is not that AI replaces traditional 3D animation outright. It is that the gap between the two approaches has narrowed enough to be worth talking about. Unreal Engine gives an artist full control over every material, camera move, and light source, which is exactly why it takes weeks to get right. AI video generation trades away some of that control in exchange for speed, letting a single creator produce something with the visual language of a car commercial, the "ultimate driving machine" energy, in a single sitting rather than a two-month production schedule.

## Key takeaways

- A 61-day Unreal Engine commercial build was used as the benchmark for a fast AI video experiment.
- The AI version, using tools like VEO 3, took 25 minutes rather than the intended 10, showing that AI video still requires real iteration.
- Even at 25 minutes, the AI approach was dramatically faster than 61 days of traditional 3D animation work.
- The test highlights a genuine tradeoff between the fine-grained control of traditional 3D tools and the speed of AI video generation.
