Automatic transcription gets you most of the way to a captioned lyric video, but "most of the way" still leaves errors, timing drift, and words that never land where they should. This tutorial from Deepali Kashab, part of the Lyrical Literacy project's production process, walks through exactly how to take a song from raw upload to a properly captioned, exported video in Descript, including the specific fixes needed when the automatic transcript gets something wrong.

## Starting with upload and automatic transcription

Descript is available both as a web app and a desktop application, and the workflow starts the same way in either: create a new video project, choose to upload a file, and select the song or music video from your desktop. Once uploaded, Descript automatically transcribes the audio, listening through the track and generating captions on its own. This step can take a little while depending on the length of the file, and once it finishes, the full transcribed lyric text appears on the left side of the screen, ready for review.

## Checking the transcript before trusting it

The first real task after transcription finishes is not adding captions, it is proofreading them. Automatic transcription makes mistakes, and the only reliable way to catch them is going through each line against the actual audio. In the walkthrough, the transcript initially rendered a lyric as "under a tree" when the actual line was "under a banyan tree," a small but real error that would have shipped into the final captions if left unchecked.

## Adding and styling captions

With the transcript reviewed, adding the visible captions is a short process: on the right side of the interface, a captions option lets you choose a font style for how the text displays over the video, with the tutorial settling on a typewriter style. Once applied, playing the video back confirms the captions are appearing and syncing roughly with the audio, which is the checkpoint before moving into detailed correction.

## Correcting words without breaking the timeline

This is the part of the workflow that matters most for anyone new to Descript: how you fix a wrong word. Double-clicking a word brings up a correction option, where you can type in the right word and confirm it. Critically, you should never try to type a brand new word directly into an empty space, since that does not work reliably. Instead, when you need to insert an additional word, like adding "banyan" before "tree," you select the existing adjacent word, use the correct option, and add the missing word as part of correcting that existing word. Deleting a word outright is also risky: removing a transcribed lyric leaves a visible dashed line in the timeline and creates a jump or small delay when the audio plays back, since the tool assumes you deleted something that was there for a timing reason. The safer approach is always to add or delete relative to an existing word rather than manipulating empty space directly, and to avoid inserting more than three or four words in a single spot at once, since larger insertions are more likely to shift the timeline out of sync.

## Fixing timing drift

Even with correct words, timing can still drift out of sync with the audio, a caption might display the right text but appear too early, too late, or not stretch to cover a longer sung word like "fortune." The fix here does not touch the underlying video or audio at all, only the caption timeline: you place your cursor on the caption, hold Command on Mac or Control on Windows, and drag it to the exact timestamp where it should appear, adjusting both when a caption starts and how long it lasts until playback confirms it lines up cleanly with the sung line.

## The three-part workflow, summarized

The entire process reduces to three checks repeated line by line: confirm the transcribed lyric text is accurate, apply and style the captions with a chosen font, and sync the caption timeline to the audio so there are no delays or jumps. It is more manual than a fully automated captioning pipeline, but it is what produces captions that actually sync to a song rather than approximately following it.

## Exporting the finished video

Once every line is corrected and synced, exporting happens from the export option in the top right corner. Descript offers two paths: a local export that saves the finished video directly to your computer, or a Descript web link that can be shared with anyone to view the same video inside Descript itself. For the local export, the recommended settings are the current composition, maximum resolution, and high quality, with no other adjustments needed before exporting.

## Key takeaways

- Descript automatically transcribes uploaded audio into captions, but the transcript needs a manual review pass against the real audio before you trust it.
- Correct a wrong word by double-clicking it and using the correct option, rather than typing directly into empty space.
- To insert a missing word, correct an adjacent existing word rather than adding a brand new one, and avoid inserting more than three or four words at once.
- Deleting a transcribed word outright can create a timeline gap and cause a playback jump; add or delete relative to existing words instead.
- Fix timing drift by holding Command (Mac) or Control (Windows) and dragging a caption to the correct timestamp on the timeline, without touching the underlying video or audio.
- Export locally for a file on your computer, set to current composition, maximum resolution, and high quality, or generate a shareable Descript web link instead.
