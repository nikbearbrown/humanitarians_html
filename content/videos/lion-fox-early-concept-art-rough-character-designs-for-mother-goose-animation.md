Before any animation studio commits to a final character look, someone has to sit with pencil and paper and simply try things. That messy, exploratory stage rarely makes it into a finished production, which is what makes this early look at Humanitarians AI's Mother Goose project worth pausing on. These are the first rough character designs for Lion and Fox, the two figures at the center of the original folk song "Face the Growl," and they show the project in its rawest, most experimental form.

## Why show unfinished work

Most creative teams only share polished results, but the rough stage is where a character's personality actually gets discovered. These paper concepts are explicitly preliminary, not the final look the animation will ship with. Sharing them is a deliberate choice to let viewers see the reasoning behind a design before it gets smoothed over, rather than only seeing the finished character once every decision has already been made.

## Building two contrasting personalities

The song "Face the Growl" pairs a curious fox against an intimidating lion, and the early sketches are already working to make that contrast visible on the page. The lion's design leans on a textured, heavily worked mane to communicate size and menace, while the fox's design is built around expressive facial features that read as alert and inquisitive. Even at this rough stage, the personalities the song calls for are starting to separate from each other visually, which is exactly what concept art at this phase is supposed to accomplish.

## Paper craft as a design tool

Rather than jumping straight to digital polish, the team is experimenting with paper craft techniques alongside color palette and proportion choices. Working this way at the earliest stage makes it cheap to try several directions for how bulky or elongated a character should be, or how saturated a color palette should read, before locking anything in for full production. It is a foundation-laying step, not a demonstration of finished art.
