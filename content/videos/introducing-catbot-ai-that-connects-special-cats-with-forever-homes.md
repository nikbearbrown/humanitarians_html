Small animal rescue nonprofits are usually run by volunteers stretched thin across adoption inquiries, medical questions, and matchmaking between cats and families, all while trying to correct misconceptions that keep the hardest-to-place animals in shelters longer than they should be. CatBot was built to take some of that load off, specifically for the cats who need it most.

Built by Humanitarians AI in collaboration with The Cat Connection and Northeastern University's D'Amore-McKim School of Business, CatBot is a chatbot designed around a specific problem: senior and special needs cats wait longer for adoption than younger, healthier animals, often due to misconceptions rather than any real barrier to a happy home. The project's framing is direct about this gap, noting that every year thousands of cats wait in shelters, and that the wait is especially long for cats that potential adopters assume will be harder to care for or shorter-lived.

## Handling the basics so people can focus on matching

At its most functional level, CatBot answers the everyday questions that would otherwise consume staff and volunteer time: shelter hours, location, adoption fees, and which cats are currently available. Automating that layer of routine information means the humans behind The Cat Connection can spend their limited time on the parts of adoption that actually require human judgment and care, rather than repeating the same logistical answers over and over.

## Educating adopters about senior and special needs cats

Beyond logistics, CatBot's more meaningful role is education. Senior cats in particular face persistent misconceptions about their health and expected lifespan that keep potential adopters from considering them. CatBot provides information and resources aimed directly at correcting those assumptions, supporting a core part of The Cat Connection's mission: helping people understand the real joys of adopting an older cat rather than defaulting to a kitten because it seems like the safer choice.

Special needs cats present a related but distinct challenge. These cats show real resilience, but adopting one successfully requires understanding how to set up a home environment that accounts for their specific conditions. CatBot helps potential adopters understand what that looks like in practice, supporting The Cat Connection's commitment to placing cats that need more considered care with families equipped to provide it.

## Matching personality to home environment

One of CatBot's more distinctive features is a personality matching quiz, engineered specifically to help identify which available cats would be a good fit for a given adopter's particular home environment. This isn't guesswork bolted onto the chatbot after the fact; it reflects insights drawn from The Cat Connection's existing foster home program, where volunteers already observe how individual cats behave and what kind of household suits them best. By encoding that foster-derived knowledge into a matching quiz, CatBot extends institutional knowledge that would otherwise live only in the heads of a small number of experienced volunteers.

## Supporting the transition home

Bringing a new cat into a household is an adjustment for any animal, and the adjustment period matters even more for senior and special needs cats. CatBot offers behavioral guidance to help smooth that transition, drawing on The Cat Connection's accumulated expertise in cat behavior. This kind of support extends the organization's reach past the moment of adoption itself, into the weeks that actually determine whether a placement succeeds long term.

## Recruiting the volunteers rescue work depends on

The Cat Connection operates as an all-volunteer organization, which means CatBot's role in recruiting volunteers and foster families is not a side feature, it is core to the whole operation staying functional. For people who are not in a position to adopt, CatBot also points toward other ways to help, including the organization's trap, neuter, return, and maintain program for feral cat populations, along with its medical support services for cat owners who cannot otherwise afford basic veterinary care.

## Measuring success differently

The project description is explicit that success for CatBot is not measured in adoption percentages or efficiency metrics in the usual business sense. It is measured, as the project puts it, in purrs, meaning every individual cat matched with a loving home counts as a win regardless of how it affects any aggregate statistic. That framing matters because it keeps the technology oriented around its actual purpose: more cats, especially the ones that wait longest, finding permanent homes.

## What The Cat Connection does

The Cat Connection itself is a nonprofit, no-kill, all-volunteer 501(c)3 organization whose mission is finding safe, loving, permanent homes for cats that have been abandoned, abused, unwanted, found as strays, neglected, or injured. Beyond adoption placement, the organization supports its broader cat community by offering veterinary and behavior consulting to cat owners who cannot afford basic care, including spay and neuter services, vaccinations, and microchipping. It also works to manage the feral cat population through trapping, neutering, and returning cats, along with socializing feral kittens so they can eventually be placed in homes.
