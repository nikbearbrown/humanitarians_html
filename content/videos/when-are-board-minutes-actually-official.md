Someone asks whether Claude can turn raw meeting notes into a full transcript of everything that was said. That's the wrong goal entirely. Minutes are the record of what was decided, not what was said, and a fuller draft isn't automatically a better one.

## Why more detail isn't the goal

It's tempting to think a fuller draft, one that captures every comment and every aside, is inherently better. But say the notes include a director's offhand doubt about a deal, later dropped without a vote. Minutes that recorded that doubt would preserve a private hesitation as a permanent record attached to a decision that was never actually made. Real board minutes leave that kind of detail out on purpose. They record decisions and actions, not discussion.

## The three-part handoff

A minutes handoff that actually works has three parts: raw notes go in, a draft limited strictly to decisions, motions, and actions comes out, and a required approval step has to happen before any of it counts as official. In practice, that means those same meeting notes come back from Claude as a clean draft, stamped in the corner as pending, not as an official record.

## Draft, then approved

That draft goes back to the board at the next meeting, gets checked line by line against what was actually decided, and only once the board votes to approve it does it become the official minutes. Two things are worth noting about that approval step. First, a draft nobody marked up isn't automatically approved: silence isn't a vote, and the board still has to say yes. Second, a draft that gets heavily corrected at approval isn't a failure of the process either. Catching an error at that stage is the approval step doing exactly what it's supposed to do.

## What actually makes minutes official

Board minutes aren't official the moment they're drafted, no matter how clean or complete they read. They're official once the board approves them. That distinction is the entire point of the handoff: a fluent draft and an official record are two different things, separated by a human check.

## Try it on a real meeting

Take rough notes from a real meeting. Ask Claude to draft board-minutes-style minutes: decisions, motions, and next steps only, not the play-by-play discussion. Then check that draft against your notes, line by line, before anyone treats it as the record.

## Key takeaways

- Board minutes record decisions and actions, not a transcript of everything that was said.
- A director's dropped comment or private doubt shouldn't appear in minutes if no vote was taken on it.
- A working handoff has three parts: raw notes in, a decisions-only draft out, and a required approval step.
- A draft nobody marked up is not automatically approved; the board still has to formally vote yes.
- Minutes become official only once the board approves them, not when Claude finishes drafting them.
