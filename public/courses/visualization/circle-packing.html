// Learn — Circle Packing 02 / 02

What this chart is and why it was chosen

What this chart is

Circle packing encodes hierarchy through containment and quantitative value through area. Each parent circle contains its children; each leaf circle's area is proportional to its data value. The perceptual mechanism is two-channel: spatial containment communicates structure (which things belong together), while area communicates magnitude (which things are larger).

It is a circular variant of the Treemap. The structural encoding — containment — is actually stronger in circle packing than in a Treemap, because nested circles make parent-child relationships spatially unambiguous. The trade-off is space efficiency: circles pack poorly, leaving significant void between them. Treemaps fill their bounding rectangle completely.

Why it was chosen here

The data has two properties that make circle packing well-suited: a clear two-level hierarchy (domains → applications) and values that need to be read in relation to their category, not in relation to a global ranking. Containment communicates the category membership immediately — no legend lookup required to associate a leaf with its parent.

The zoomable interaction resolves the space-efficiency problem: at the top level, domain sizes are the story. Zooming in makes individual application sizes legible without requiring a separate chart.

What the alternative would break

A Treemap would show the same hierarchy with better space efficiency and more accurate area comparison (rectangles are easier to compare than circles). The trade-off: hierarchical structure is harder to read. When branches interlock in a Treemap, the parent boundary can be ambiguous — especially at three or more levels of depth. Circle packing makes nesting unambiguous at the cost of whitespace.

A grouped bar chart would show the magnitude comparison accurately but would lose all hierarchical containment encoding — the relationship between domain and application becomes a visual lookup task.

Honest limitations

Circle packing is among the least space-efficient chart types. Area comparison is also one of the weaker perceptual channels — humans are poor at judging the ratio between two circles of similar size. The chart compensates with interaction (click to zoom, hover for exact values) and redundant color encoding by category.

If precise magnitude comparison is the primary goal, a bar chart or dot plot will outperform circle packing every time. This chart earns its place when hierarchy revelation matters as much as magnitude comparison.

// Framework Reference

FT Visual Vocabulary category: Part-to-whole — Hierarchy. Abela quadrant: Composition (how a whole breaks into parts, with hierarchical structure). The design decision worth knowing: color is applied at depth 1 (the category level), not the leaf level. Leaves inherit their parent's hue. This means color encodes membership, not a third variable — keeping the encoding honest. Adding a third variable via color (e.g., saturation for performance) is possible but risks overloading a chart that already carries two visual channels.
